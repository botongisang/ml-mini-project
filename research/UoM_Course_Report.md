# Comprehensive Course Report: University of Melbourne — Six Undergraduate Configurations

## Critical Structural Clarification (Read Before Proceeding)

The six course configurations listed in the brief require an important factual correction before analysis begins.

**The University of Melbourne does not currently offer undergraduate double degrees.** When the university introduced the Melbourne Model in 2008, it eliminated its 96 traditional undergraduate courses and replaced them with six broad undergraduate degrees. Concurrent double degrees — where a student enrols in two separate degree programs simultaneously — were discontinued. A proposal to reintroduce them under "Melbourne Model 2.0" was floated in 2018 but was not implemented as of 2025.

What the university does offer is:
- A **double major** within a single degree (e.g., Management + Marketing double major within the Bachelor of Commerce — completed in three years at no extra cost)
- **Concurrent diplomas** alongside a bachelor degree (not full double degrees)
- A breadth component (50 credit points) within any bachelor degree that allows study in another faculty's subjects

Consequently:
- "BCom + Bachelor of Arts (International Relations)" as a formal double degree does **not exist** at UoM.
- "BCom + Bachelor of Media and Communications" as a formal double degree does **not exist** at UoM.
- "Bachelor of Media and Communications" as a standalone degree does **not exist** — Media and Communications is a **major within the Bachelor of Arts (B-ARTS)**.
- "Digital Business" is **not a standalone major or specialisation in 2025**. From 2026, the BCom introduced 10 specialisations; the closest is the **Digital Marketing** specialisation within the Marketing major. There is no specialisation specifically named "Digital Business."

This report interprets the six configurations as follows and covers each honestly:

| # | Configuration as Listed | What Actually Exists at UoM |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | BCom (Digital Business + Business double major) | BCom with Management + Marketing double major (closest match to "digital business" + "business") |
| 2 | BCom (Digital Business specialisation) | BCom with Marketing major + Digital Marketing specialisation (from 2026) |
| 3 | BCom + Bachelor of Media and Communications (double degree) | BCom with breadth subjects in Media & Communications (formal double degree does not exist) |
| 4 | BCom (Business specialisation) | BCom with Management major (the core "business" major) |
| 5 | BCom + Bachelor of Arts (International Relations) (double degree) | BCom with breadth subjects in Politics & International Studies (formal double degree does not exist) |
| 6 | Bachelor of Media and Communications | Bachelor of Arts with Media and Communications major |

Each course below is reported on its actual structure. Where a listed configuration cannot exist as described, the report explains what the closest available option is and what is realistically achievable.

# Course 1: BCom — Management + Marketing Double Major (Closest Match to "Digital Business + Business")

## 1. Course Overview

**Official Full Name and Course Code:** Bachelor of Commerce (B-COM). The double major in Management and Marketing is a study path within this single degree. There is no separate course code for the major combination.

**Duration and Total Credit Points:** 3 years full-time (72 months part-time). 300 credit points total.

**Faculty/School:** Faculty of Business and Economics, University of Melbourne.

**Campus and Delivery Mode:** Parkville campus, Melbourne. On-campus, in-person delivery. No fully online version exists for domestic students.

**Entry Requirements:**
- ATAR: Guaranteed entry ATAR of 92.00 for 2026 intake (lowest selection rank in 2025 was 92.00, guide only). In practice, competition frequently pushes effective entry above 95.
- VCE prerequisites: English study score of at least 25 (or 30 in EAL); Mathematics Methods or Specialist Mathematics study score of at least 25.
- IB: Minimum grade 4 at HL or SL in English Language A (Literature or Language & Literature, or English B for non-native speakers); minimum grade 4 at HL or SL in Mathematics (Analysis and Approaches, or grade 4 at HL in Mathematics Applications and Interpretations).
- English Language (non-VCE/IB): Undergraduate English language requirements must be satisfied (IELTS 6.5 overall with no band below 6.0, or equivalent).

**Annual Tuition Fees:**
- Domestic students: Commonwealth Supported Place (CSP). Commerce subjects fall under Band 4 (previously called the highest band under the pre-2021 system). The 2025 maximum annual student contribution for Band 4 subjects (law, accounting, economics, commerce) is approximately **AUD 10,440 per year** for a full-time load, giving a total CSP cost of approximately **AUD 31,320 over three years**. HECS-HELP is available to eligible citizens and permanent residents to defer this contribution.
- International students: Approximately **AUD 44,512 per year** (indicative, per university data for 2025). Total indicative cost: approximately **AUD 133,536 over three years**. Source: University of Melbourne indicative international fee schedules.

**HECS-HELP Band:** Commerce and business subjects fall under **Band 4** (the highest student contribution band, set by the Australian Government). The Band 4 maximum student contribution in 2025 is **AUD 10,440 per EFTSL** (Equivalent Full-Time Student Load). A full-time BCom student takes 8 subjects per year (100 points), so the annual contribution is approximately AUD 10,440 × 1.0 EFTSL = **~AUD 10,440/year**, or **~AUD 31,320 total** over three years under HECS-HELP. Source: Study Assist, Australian Government 2025 student contribution amounts.

**Available Intakes:** Semester 1 (February/March) only for first-year domestic students. Semester 2 (July) entry is available for some international applicants subject to course availability. Mid-year entry is restricted and not guaranteed for all major combinations.

## 4. Pros

1. **Elite employer reputation.** The University of Melbourne ranked #1 in Australia for employer reputation (QS 2025 World University Rankings). MBB and Big 4 firms actively recruit from this campus, giving graduates privileged access to Australia's most competitive graduate programs.

2. **Double major at no extra cost or time.** Completing Management + Marketing within the standard 300-point three-year BCom adds no time and no additional fees compared to a single major. This is a material differentiator versus, say, a four-year double degree at other universities.

3. **Breadth component creates genuine cross-disciplinary depth.** The 50 credit points of compulsory breadth study allows students to build real knowledge in computing, law, media, languages, or politics — providing rounding that a purely business curriculum cannot offer.

4. **Digital Marketing specialisation available from 2026.** Students entering from 2026 can combine their Marketing major with the Digital Marketing specialisation (25 Level 3 credit points), formally structuring the digital-specific learning that was previously only available through elective selection.

5. **Practical consulting and internship pathways embedded in the degree.** Subjects like MGMT30012 Management Consulting and MGMT30017 Global Management Consulting, plus the BCom Internship program and WorkHub, provide structured real-world industry exposure during the degree itself.

6. **Marketing major graduates receive automatic AMI associate membership.** This professional recognition has tangible value on a CV and opens access to AMI resources, events, and networks immediately upon graduation.

7. **Strong digital marketing subject offering.** MKTG30009 Digital Marketing, MKTG30014 Data Driven Marketing, and MKTG30010 Advertising and Promotions form a practical, current digital skills cluster rarely matched at this calibre in a commerce degree.

8. **Broad career optionality.** The Management + Marketing double major opens paths into consulting, brand management, digital marketing, e-commerce, strategy, operations, HR, and entrepreneurship — among the widest career optionality of any BCom configuration at UoM.

9. **Global exchange opportunities.** The Global Semester program provides access to leading universities worldwide, with exchange subject credit accepted toward the degree.

10. **QS subject ranking #27 globally for Social Sciences and Management (2025).** The Faculty of Business and Economics is internationally recognised, which matters for multinational employer shortlisting.

## 5. Cons

1. **"Digital Business" is not a formally named specialisation in 2025.** Students intending to study "Digital Business" will find that no such named program exists. The closest is Marketing + Digital Marketing specialisation (from 2026). Students who enrolled expecting this specific label will need to construct it through careful subject selection without institutional guidance structured around that title.

2. **No honours pathway in Management or Marketing.** Unlike Accounting, Actuarial Studies, Economics, and Finance, the Management and Marketing majors at UoM do not have a direct honours year pathway. This limits the ability to use these majors as a direct springboard to a PhD in management or marketing without first completing a graduate degree.

3. **Highly competitive entry.** The 92.00 ATAR guarantee is a floor, not the median offer. In practice, competition for places means effective entry is regularly in the mid-90s. Students narrowly meeting the guarantee may still miss out in competitive years.

4. **Breadth component can dilute focus.** While breadth is a structural advantage, students who want to go deep in management and marketing will find that 50 of their 300 credit points are mandatorily spent outside the faculty. This compresses the volume of major elective choices available.

5. **Limited embedded work-integrated learning hours.** The BCom Internship requires only 90 hours minimum — considerably shorter than the 200–450 hour practicums embedded in some competitor programs (e.g., Monash's co-op model). Students seeking extensive work experience during their degree must actively self-source it.

6. **Semester 2 entry is not guaranteed.** Most BCom students enter in Semester 1 only, limiting flexibility for school leavers or gap-year students who complete VCE mid-cycle.

7. **Subject availability can constrain double major planning.** Several Level 3 electives are offered in only one semester per year. Students in a Management + Marketing double major must plan carefully to avoid clashes, particularly if they also want a specialisation from 2026.

6. **Competition for consulting roles is very high.** While this major is explicitly designed to prepare students for consulting, the graduate consulting market in Australia is among the most competitive. Acceptance rates at MBB firms for Australian university applicants are estimated at 1–3%. ATAR and GPA are important but not sufficient.

## 4. Pros

4. **DFAT Graduate Program appeal.** The Australian Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade Graduate Program is one of Australia's most selective (approximately 30–50 places per year from thousands of applicants). The BCom Economics/Management + International Relations breadth profile is precisely the profile DFAT looks for: commercial literacy, policy reasoning, global awareness, and language capability.

5. **Political risk analysis is a growing private sector need.** Multinational corporations, financial institutions, and infrastructure investors increasingly employ political risk analysts. The BCom + IR combination directly prepares for this niche, which typically earns AUD 100,000+ at mid-career.

6. **Strong alignment with Australia's growing Asia-Pacific strategic priorities.** Subjects like Australian Foreign Policy and Chinese Politics and Society are directly relevant as Australia navigates its economic and strategic relationships in the region.

7. **Breadth subjects in Politics are taught by active researchers.** UoM's School of Social and Political Sciences is a research-active unit with faculty who publish on Australian foreign policy, Asian politics, and international security. Undergraduate breadth students benefit from this research environment.

8. **No additional time or cost.** The combination is achievable within the standard 3-year, 300-point BCom with no extra fees.

## 5. Cons

1. **No formal "International Relations" credential on the transcript.** The BCom graduate receives only a Bachelor of Commerce. Employers or postgraduate programs that require or prefer a formal BA in Politics or International Studies will see this student as a commerce graduate, not an international relations graduate.

2. **DFAT competitiveness is extremely high.** The DFAT Graduate Program is one of Australia's most selective, and the lack of a formal IR degree (even with breadth) can be a disadvantage in a field where a Master of International Relations from ANU or Melbourne is the typical competitor profile.

3. **Breadth credit points can cover only half of a full Politics & International Studies major.** 50 credit points represents roughly half the major requirement (100 credit points). Students cannot claim the major on their transcript.

4. **Language skills are not formally embedded.** Successful diplomatic and international careers strongly benefit from foreign language competency. This BCom configuration does not formally require or structure language learning, and students must self-organise language study through elective or breadth subject selection (which may compete with other priorities).

5. **Entry into international organisations typically requires postgraduate study.** Roles at the World Bank, IMF, UN, or OECD almost universally require a Master's degree. The BCom + IR breadth combination is a good foundation but not a terminal credential for this career path.

6. **Government graduate salaries are relatively modest.** APS Graduate Program salaries start at approximately AUD 58,000–68,000 — below the commerce national median of AUD 72,000. Students attracted by the prestige of government/diplomatic careers should understand the initial salary trade-off.

Undergraduates who take initiative through the School of Social and Political Sciences can access speakers, events, and research internship opportunities that are uniquely available at this institution.

# Course 6: Bachelor of Arts — Media and Communications Major

## 1. Course Overview

**Factual Clarification:** The University of Melbourne does not offer a standalone "Bachelor of Media and Communications." Media and Communications is a **major within the Bachelor of Arts (B-ARTS)**. Students who want to study Media and Communications at UoM enrol in the Bachelor of Arts and select Media and Communications as their major (or extended major).

**Official Full Name and Course Code:** Bachelor of Arts (B-ARTS), with Media and Communications major. Course code: B-ARTS.

**Duration and Total Credit Points:** 3 years full-time (72 months part-time). 300 credit points total.

**Faculty/School:** Faculty of Arts, School of Culture and Communication, University of Melbourne.

**Campus and Delivery Mode:** Parkville campus, Melbourne. On-campus, in-person delivery. All classes are delivered on-campus at Parkville across Semesters 1 and 2. Note: Media and Communications classes include studio-based, laboratory, and seminar formats — not lecture-only.

**Entry Requirements:**
- ATAR: Guaranteed ATAR **88.00** for 2026 intake (some sources note 83.00 for international applicants). Lowest selection rank for 2025: **86.00** (guide only). Note: updated ATAR guarantee for 2026–2027 has been reported at 83 for international applicants (lowered by 2 points).
- VCE prerequisites: English study score of at least 25 in English, English Language, or Literature (or 30 in EAL). No specific Mathematics prerequisite for Bachelor of Arts.
- IB: Minimum grade 4 at HL or SL in English Language A (or equivalent).
- English Language (non-VCE/IB): Undergraduate English language requirements must be satisfied.
- No subject-specific prerequisites beyond English. This is a key difference from the BCom.

**Annual Tuition Fees:**
- Domestic students (CSP): Arts and humanities subjects fall primarily under **Band 2** (humanities, behavioural science, social studies, languages, visual and performing arts). The 2025 maximum student contribution for Band 2 is approximately **AUD 6,256 per year** for a full-time load. Total over 3 years: approximately **AUD 18,768**. Note: Some Media & Communications subjects involving computing or communications technology may attract Band 3 contributions. The blended annual contribution is likely in the range of AUD 6,000–7,500/year.
- International students: approximately **AUD 38,400/year** (indicative 2025 for Bachelor of Arts). Total indicative cost: approximately **AUD 115,200 over three years**. Source: University of Melbourne indicative international fee schedule.

**HECS-HELP Band:** Primarily **Band 2** (~AUD 6,256/year maximum, 2025). Total domestic HECS-HELP debt over 3 years: approximately **AUD 18,768** — significantly lower than the BCom.

## 4. Pros

1. **Lower entry barrier than BCom.** ATAR guarantee of 88.00 (vs. 92.00 for BCom) and no Mathematics prerequisite makes the B-ARTS more accessible. Students who are passionate about media and communication but do not meet the Mathematics requirement for BCom can pursue this path.

2. **Lowest total HECS-HELP debt of any configuration reviewed.** Total domestic student contribution of approximately AUD 18,768 over 3 years is nearly 40% less than the BCom's ~AUD 31,320. For domestic students using HECS-HELP, this represents a significantly lower long-term debt.

3. **Faculty expertise in critical media theory.** UoM's School of Culture and Communication has faculty who research digital media, journalism ethics, platform governance, and global media flows. Undergraduate students benefit from this research-active environment in a way that is not available at most media programs at Australian universities.

4. **Global Media Cultures capstone (MECM30002)** provides a sophisticated theoretical lens on globalisation and digital media that is genuinely useful for careers in international media organisations, policy, or research.

5. **Media Communications Project (MECM30015)** allows students to produce substantial, portfolio-ready work — whether a multimedia journalism piece, documentary, or research paper — that is directly presentable to employers.

6. **Semester 1 and 2 entry available.** Greater intake flexibility than the BCom, accommodating students with non-standard application timelines.

7. **Wide second major options broaden career positioning.** Pairing Media and Communications with Politics & International Studies (for political journalism or government communications), Psychology (for communications effectiveness research), or Economics (for business journalism) creates distinct, specialised graduate profiles.

8. **Strong foundation for postgraduate specialisation.** The B-ARTS Media and Communications + Master of Journalism pathway is widely regarded as the best journalism training pathway in Melbourne, and the combined credential is more competitive for major masthead roles than a bachelor's degree alone.

## 5. Cons

1. **Lower average starting salaries than BCom configurations.** The national median starting salary for humanities graduates (AUD 69,400) is approximately AUD 2,600–5,600 below the business and management median (AUD 72,000–75,000). Journalism award rates can be significantly lower, starting around AUD 55,000–60,000 for cadet journalists.

2. **No mandatory industry placement embedded.** Unlike some competitor media programs (e.g., RMIT's journalism program with compulsory newsroom placements), the UoM Media and Communications major does not require formal industry placement. Students who want practical experience must self-source it — which requires significant initiative and networking.

3. **Traditional journalism industry is structurally declining.** Masthead newsrooms in Australia have shed substantial staffing. The ABC, Fairfax/Nine, News Corp, and Seven all reduced editorial headcount significantly over 2015–2024. Students entering journalism face a competitive and contracting job market at traditional outlets, even as digital content creation grows.

4. **The degree title "Bachelor of Arts" can create perception challenges.** Some employers outside the media and communications sector have historically undervalued Arts degrees relative to Commerce or Science degrees. This perception, while often inaccurate, can affect shortlisting in corporate environments.

5. **Practical production skills may be less developed than at specialist media programs.** RMIT, Swinburne, and Monash offer dedicated media degrees with dedicated studios, newsrooms, and broadcast facilities. UoM's media program is more academically oriented. Students seeking hands-on production training may find those programs provide better practical preparation.

6. **Lower employer reputation premium than BCom for non-media sectors.** UoM's #1 employer reputation is less directly impactful for journalism and PR roles, where portfolio quality and practical experience matter more than institutional prestige.

# Comparative Analysis

## Priority Order by Future Career Scope (2025–2035)

The following ranking assesses all six configurations from highest to lowest future career potential, factoring in automation risk, AI/tech industry growth trends, global demand, salary trajectory, and career versatility.

---

**Rank 1 — BCom: Management + Marketing Double Major (Course 1)**

This configuration offers the broadest and most commercially grounded career optionality of the six. The Management + Marketing double major produces graduates who can enter consulting, digital marketing, brand management, strategy, HR, operations, and entrepreneurship — covering the full width of Australia's fastest-growing private sector roles. The Digital Marketing and Data Driven Marketing subjects directly address the most automation-resistant aspects of marketing work (strategic creativity, audience psychology, data interpretation). The consulting pipeline through MGMT30012 and MGMT30017 opens access to MBB and Big 4 graduate programs, which offer the highest graduate salaries of any commerce pathway. The Future Skills Organisation's projection of 3.5 million finance, technology, and business professionals needed in Australia by 2030 — up 450,000 from today — directly benefits this profile. The combination of people leadership (Management) and digital commercial strategy (Marketing) is among the most resilient to automation displacement.

---

**Rank 2 — BCom: Marketing Major + Digital Marketing Specialisation (Course 2)**

Digital marketing is one of the fastest-growing skill categories in Australia and globally. The QILT 2024 data confirms that business and management graduates are above the national starting salary median, and digital marketing specialists command a premium within that category. AI tools are automating some mechanical marketing tasks (scheduling, A/B testing, basic copy generation) but are expanding demand for humans who can interpret data, set strategy, and manage brand identity — exactly what the UoM Marketing curriculum prepares for. The Digital Marketing specialisation from 2026 formalises this positioning. The single-major structure leaves more breadth points for technical computing subjects, which is the strongest differentiation a student can build. Mid-career ceiling (AUD 90,000–180,000 for digital marketing leadership) is strong. The main risk is that without a management or strategy counterpart, the profile can appear narrower than Course 1. Placed second because it is a strong, focused career path but slightly less versatile than the double major.

---

**Rank 3 — BCom: Management Major (Course 4)**

The pure Management major is the strongest direct pipeline to management consulting — the highest-paid graduate career accessible from a three-year BCom. The consulting market in Australia is projected to grow substantially through 2035, driven by digital transformation, ESG advisory, and organisational redesign in response to AI. MGMT30019 The Future of Work and MGMT30008 Managing Sustainably address exactly the macro trends that will dominate corporate agendas through 2035. The risk is that management as a discipline is perceived as relatively "soft" in analytical industries, and the lack of an honours pathway in Management (versus Finance or Economics) limits the PhD research pipeline. However, for students targeting consulting, operations, and strategy careers, this is a strong and directed choice. Placed third because it is excellent for consulting but narrower than the double-major configuration in cross-industry optionality.

---

**Rank 4 — BCom + Politics & International Studies Breadth Combination (Course 5)**

The international business and diplomacy career path is growing in relevance as Australia's geopolitical and trade environment becomes more complex, particularly in the Indo-Pacific region. The Future Skills Organisation explicitly identifies international trade and cross-cultural management as growing skill areas. The BCom + IR breadth profile is distinctive and under-supplied — few graduates in Australia have both rigorous commercial training and genuine international relations knowledge. The salary ceiling in government/diplomacy is lower at entry but grows significantly with seniority. Political risk analysis and government affairs management are AUD 100,000–180,000 mid-career roles. The limitation is that the most prestigious IR career paths (DFAT, multilateral organisations) strongly prefer postgraduate credentials, so this configuration works best as a foundation for a subsequent Master of International Relations. Placed fourth because it is highly distinctive and growing in relevance but less immediately commercially lucrative than the above three.

---

**Rank 5 — BCom + Media Communications Breadth Combination (Course 3)**

The communications and PR sector is growing, driven by corporate demand for reputation management, ESG communications, and digital content. The automation risk for strategic communications (stakeholder management, narrative framing, crisis response) is lower than for routine marketing tasks. However, this configuration's principal limitation is that it does not produce a recognised Media and Communications credential — graduates hold only a BCom. The 50-point breadth covers half a media major, which is meaningful but not employer-equivalent to a full Arts or Communications degree. Placed fifth because the career outcomes are strong for brand communications and PR but the credential gap limits penetration into dedicated media industry roles.

---

**Rank 6 — Bachelor of Arts: Media and Communications Major (Course 6)**

The B-ARTS with Media and Communications major has the most limited commercial career optionality of the six configurations, particularly in the near term. Traditional journalism is a structurally declining sector. PR and communications roles are achievable but carry the lowest entry salaries of the group. The degree's long-term value is real — communications directors and senior PR professionals earn AUD 130,000–200,000+ — but the path is longer, more competitive for high-paying roles, and more subject to sector disruption (AI content generation threatens entry-level content and copy roles). The degree's research orientation is an intellectual strength but not immediately commercially valorised. The lowest HECS-HELP debt partially compensates, and the postgraduate journalism pathway at UoM is genuinely excellent. Placed sixth not because the degree lacks merit, but because within this specific comparative set it offers the most restricted immediate commercial optionality and the lowest starting salary trajectory.

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### Digital Business (Course 2) vs. Digital Business + Business Double Major (Course 1): Which Is Better and For Whom?

The Management + Marketing double major (Course 1) is objectively more powerful for the majority of students, with one important exception.

The double major produces graduates who can simultaneously speak the languages of organisational strategy (consulting, HR, operations) and commercial marketing (brand, digital, growth). For roles in management consulting, general management, brand strategy, or corporate strategy, this breadth is an active advantage. The management consulting firms (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte, PwC) that dominate graduate hiring from UoM prefer candidates who can demonstrate analytical rigour and strategic breadth — the double major signals this more clearly than a single marketing specialisation.

The single Marketing + Digital Marketing specialisation (Course 2) is better for the student who is certain about a digital-specific career and wants to use their freed breadth points for technical subjects (Information Systems, Data Science, Statistics, Computing) that deepen their analytical toolkit. A Marketing + Digital Marketing + computing breadth profile can be more compelling to digital-native companies (Canva, Atlassian, HubSpot, Adobe) than the generalist double major.

**Choose Course 1 (double major) if:** You are uncertain about your exact specialisation, want access to consulting pathways, and value versatility.

**Choose Course 2 (single marketing + digital specialisation) if:** You are certain you want a digital marketing or marketing technology career and plan to use breadth points for computing and data science subjects.

---

### BCom Management (Course 4) vs. BCom + Media Communications Breadth (Course 3): When Does the Media Breadth Pay Off?

The media breadth pays off in three specific career scenarios:
1. You want to work in corporate communications, PR, or reputation management for a major corporation. The commerce + media combination is rare and directly addresses what these teams need.
2. You want to work at the intersection of marketing and content — as a brand storyteller, content strategist, or integrated campaigns manager. Media theory plus marketing training is the ideal preparation.
3. You want to work in government communications or policy communications. The commercial grounding from BCom combined with media knowledge is the exact profile government communications units seek.

For all other career paths — management consulting, general management, HR, operations, strategy — the pure Management major (Course 4) is more efficient. Spending 50 breadth points on media subjects when you want a consulting career is an opportunity cost, as those points could go to Economics, Computing, Law, or other subjects that strengthen analytical and commercial credentials.

**Choose Course 4 (Management only) if:** Your target is consulting, general management, HR, operations, or strategy.

**Choose Course 3 (Management + Media breadth) if:** Your target is corporate communications, integrated marketing, PR, or any role that sits at the intersection of commercial and communications functions.

---

### BCom + International Relations Breadth (Course 5) vs. Bachelor of Arts Media and Communications (Course 6): Which Has Broader Scope?

Course 5 (BCom + IR breadth) has broader long-term career scope for the following reasons:

Commerce training opens doors that a pure arts degree does not — investment banking, consulting, FMCG, and corporate strategy are all accessible to a BCom graduate, but very few of these roles actively recruit B-ARTS graduates. The IR breadth adds a distinctive differentiator without sacrificing commercial employability.

The B-ARTS Media (Course 6) has scope that is more sector-specific — it is deep within media, communications, and journalism but narrow in cross-sector commercial applicability. The journalism market is structurally declining. The PR/communications market is growing but has a lower salary ceiling than most BCom career paths.

Where Course 6 wins over Course 5 is in direct entry to journalism and specialist media roles. An employer at the ABC or Guardian Australia looking for a journalist or content producer sees the B-ARTS + Media major as a better credential than a BCom with IR breadth. For this specific career pathway, Course 6 is more appropriate.

**Choose Course 5 if:** You want the maximum career optionality across commercial, diplomatic, government, and international business sectors.

**Choose Course 6 if:** You are certain you want to work in journalism, media production, or communications in a media-sector-specific environment.

## 5. Cons

1. **No formal "International Relations" credential on the transcript.** The BCom graduate receives only a Bachelor of Commerce. Employers or postgraduate programs that require or prefer a formal BA in Politics or International Studies will see this student as a commerce graduate, not an international relations graduate.

2. **DFAT competitiveness is extremely high.** The DFAT Graduate Program is one of Australia's most selective, and the lack of a formal IR degree (even with breadth) can be a disadvantage in a field where a Master of International Relations from ANU or Melbourne is the typical competitor profile.

3. **Breadth credit points can cover only half of a full Politics & International Studies major.** 50 credit points represents roughly half the major requirement (100 credit points). Students cannot claim the major on their transcript.

4. **Language skills are not formally embedded.** Successful diplomatic and international careers strongly benefit from foreign language competency. This BCom configuration does not formally require or structure language learning, and students must self-organise language study through elective or breadth subject selection (which may compete with other priorities).

5. **Entry into international organisations typically requires postgraduate study.** Roles at the World Bank, IMF, UN, or OECD almost universally require a Master's degree. The BCom + IR breadth combination is a good foundation but not a terminal credential for this career path.

6. **Government graduate salaries are relatively modest.** APS Graduate Program salaries start at approximately AUD 58,000–68,000 — below the commerce national median of AUD 72,000. Students attracted by the prestige of government/diplomatic careers should understand the initial salary trade-off.

# Course 6: Bachelor of Arts — Media and Communications Major

## 1. Course Overview

**Factual Clarification:** The University of Melbourne does not offer a standalone "Bachelor of Media and Communications." Media and Communications is a **major within the Bachelor of Arts (B-ARTS)**. Students who want to study Media and Communications at UoM enrol in the Bachelor of Arts and select Media and Communications as their major (or extended major).

**Official Full Name and Course Code:** Bachelor of Arts (B-ARTS), with Media and Communications major. Course code: B-ARTS.

**Duration and Total Credit Points:** 3 years full-time (72 months part-time). 300 credit points total.

**Faculty/School:** Faculty of Arts, School of Culture and Communication, University of Melbourne.

**Campus and Delivery Mode:** Parkville campus, Melbourne. On-campus, in-person delivery. All classes are delivered on-campus at Parkville across Semesters 1 and 2. Note: Media and Communications classes include studio-based, laboratory, and seminar formats — not lecture-only.

**Entry Requirements:**
- ATAR: Guaranteed ATAR **88.00** for 2026 intake (some sources note 83.00 for international applicants). Lowest selection rank for 2025: **86.00** (guide only). Note: updated ATAR guarantee for 2026–2027 has been reported at 83 for international applicants (lowered by 2 points).
- VCE prerequisites: English study score of at least 25 in English, English Language, or Literature (or 30 in EAL). No specific Mathematics prerequisite for Bachelor of Arts.
- IB: Minimum grade 4 at HL or SL in English Language A (or equivalent).
- English Language (non-VCE/IB): Undergraduate English language requirements must be satisfied.
- No subject-specific prerequisites beyond English. This is a key difference from the BCom.

**Annual Tuition Fees:**
- Domestic students (CSP): Arts and humanities subjects fall primarily under **Band 2** (humanities, behavioural science, social studies, languages, visual and performing arts). The 2025 maximum student contribution for Band 2 is approximately **AUD 6,256 per year** for a full-time load. Total over 3 years: approximately **AUD 18,768**. Note: Some Media & Communications subjects involving computing or communications technology may attract Band 3 contributions. The blended annual contribution is likely in the range of AUD 6,000–7,500/year.
- International students: approximately **AUD 38,400/year** (indicative 2025 for Bachelor of Arts). Total indicative cost: approximately **AUD 115,200 over three years**. Source: University of Melbourne indicative international fee schedule.

**HECS-HELP Band:** Primarily **Band 2** (~AUD 6,256/year maximum, 2025). Total domestic HECS-HELP debt over 3 years: approximately **AUD 18,768** — significantly lower than the BCom.

**Available Intakes:** Semester 1 and Semester 2 entry generally available for the Bachelor of Arts, giving greater flexibility than the BCom.

# Comparative Analysis

## Priority Order by Future Career Scope (2025–2035)

The following ranking assesses all six configurations from highest to lowest future career potential, factoring in automation risk, AI/tech industry growth trends, global demand, salary trajectory, and career versatility.

---

**Rank 1 — BCom: Management + Marketing Double Major (Course 1)**

This configuration offers the broadest and most commercially grounded career optionality of the six. The Management + Marketing double major produces graduates who can enter consulting, digital marketing, brand management, strategy, HR, operations, and entrepreneurship — covering the full width of Australia's fastest-growing private sector roles. The Digital Marketing and Data Driven Marketing subjects directly address the most automation-resistant aspects of marketing work (strategic creativity, audience psychology, data interpretation). The consulting pipeline through MGMT30012 and MGMT30017 opens access to MBB and Big 4 graduate programs, which offer the highest graduate salaries of any commerce pathway. The Future Skills Organisation's projection of 3.5 million finance, technology, and business professionals needed in Australia by 2030 — up 450,000 from today — directly benefits this profile. The combination of people leadership (Management) and digital commercial strategy (Marketing) is among the most resilient to automation displacement.

---

**Rank 2 — BCom: Marketing Major + Digital Marketing Specialisation (Course 2)**

Digital marketing is one of the fastest-growing skill categories in Australia and globally. The QILT 2024 data confirms that business and management graduates are above the national starting salary median, and digital marketing specialists command a premium within that category. AI tools are automating some mechanical marketing tasks (scheduling, A/B testing, basic copy generation) but are expanding demand for humans who can interpret data, set strategy, and manage brand identity — exactly what the UoM Marketing curriculum prepares for. The Digital Marketing specialisation from 2026 formalises this positioning. The single-major structure leaves more breadth points for technical computing subjects, which is the strongest differentiation a student can build. Mid-career ceiling (AUD 90,000–180,000 for digital marketing leadership) is strong. The main risk is that without a management or strategy counterpart, the profile can appear narrower than Course 1. Placed second because it is a strong, focused career path but slightly less versatile than the double major.

---

**Rank 3 — BCom: Management Major (Course 4)**

The pure Management major is the strongest direct pipeline to management consulting — the highest-paid graduate career accessible from a three-year BCom. The consulting market in Australia is projected to grow substantially through 2035, driven by digital transformation, ESG advisory, and organisational redesign in response to AI. MGMT30019 The Future of Work and MGMT30008 Managing Sustainably address exactly the macro trends that will dominate corporate agendas through 2035. The risk is that management as a discipline is perceived as relatively "soft" in analytical industries, and the lack of an honours pathway in Management (versus Finance or Economics) limits the PhD research pipeline. However, for students targeting consulting, operations, and strategy careers, this is a strong and directed choice. Placed third because it is excellent for consulting but narrower than the double-major configuration in cross-industry optionality.

---

**Rank 4 — BCom + Politics & International Studies Breadth Combination (Course 5)**

The international business and diplomacy career path is growing in relevance as Australia's geopolitical and trade environment becomes more complex, particularly in the Indo-Pacific region. The Future Skills Organisation explicitly identifies international trade and cross-cultural management as growing skill areas. The BCom + IR breadth profile is distinctive and under-supplied — few graduates in Australia have both rigorous commercial training and genuine international relations knowledge. The salary ceiling in government/diplomacy is lower at entry but grows significantly with seniority. Political risk analysis and government affairs management are AUD 100,000–180,000 mid-career roles. The limitation is that the most prestigious IR career paths (DFAT, multilateral organisations) strongly prefer postgraduate credentials, so this configuration works best as a foundation for a subsequent Master of International Relations. Placed fourth because it is highly distinctive and growing in relevance but less immediately commercially lucrative than the above three.

---

**Rank 5 — BCom + Media Communications Breadth Combination (Course 3)**

The communications and PR sector is growing, driven by corporate demand for reputation management, ESG communications, and digital content. The automation risk for strategic communications (stakeholder management, narrative framing, crisis response) is lower than for routine marketing tasks. However, this configuration's principal limitation is that it does not produce a recognised Media and Communications credential — graduates hold only a BCom. The 50-point breadth covers half a media major, which is meaningful but not employer-equivalent to a full Arts or Communications degree. Placed fifth because the career outcomes are strong for brand communications and PR but the credential gap limits penetration into dedicated media industry roles.

---

**Rank 6 — Bachelor of Arts: Media and Communications Major (Course 6)**

The B-ARTS with Media and Communications major has the most limited commercial career optionality of the six configurations, particularly in the near term. Traditional journalism is a structurally declining sector. PR and communications roles are achievable but carry the lowest entry salaries of the group. The degree's long-term value is real — communications directors and senior PR professionals earn AUD 130,000–200,000+ — but the path is longer, more competitive for high-paying roles, and more subject to sector disruption (AI content generation threatens entry-level content and copy roles). The degree's research orientation is an intellectual strength but not immediately commercially valorised. The lowest HECS-HELP debt partially compensates, and the postgraduate journalism pathway at UoM is genuinely excellent. Placed sixth not because the degree lacks merit, but because within this specific comparative set it offers the most restricted immediate commercial optionality and the lowest starting salary trajectory.

## Head-to-Head Comparisons

### Digital Business (Course 2) vs. Digital Business + Business Double Major (Course 1): Which Is Better and For Whom?

The Management + Marketing double major (Course 1) is objectively more powerful for the majority of students, with one important exception.

The double major produces graduates who can simultaneously speak the languages of organisational strategy (consulting, HR, operations) and commercial marketing (brand, digital, growth). For roles in management consulting, general management, brand strategy, or corporate strategy, this breadth is an active advantage. The management consulting firms (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte, PwC) that dominate graduate hiring from UoM prefer candidates who can demonstrate analytical rigour and strategic breadth — the double major signals this more clearly than a single marketing specialisation.

The single Marketing + Digital Marketing specialisation (Course 2) is better for the student who is certain about a digital-specific career and wants to use their freed breadth points for technical subjects (Information Systems, Data Science, Statistics, Computing) that deepen their analytical toolkit. A Marketing + Digital Marketing + computing breadth profile can be more compelling to digital-native companies (Canva, Atlassian, HubSpot, Adobe) than the generalist double major.

**Choose Course 1 (double major) if:** You are uncertain about your exact specialisation, want access to consulting pathways, and value versatility.

**Choose Course 2 (single marketing + digital specialisation) if:** You are certain you want a digital marketing or marketing technology career and plan to use breadth points for computing and data science subjects.

---

### BCom Management (Course 4) vs. BCom + Media Communications Breadth (Course 3): When Does the Media Breadth Pay Off?

The media breadth pays off in three specific career scenarios:
1. You want to work in corporate communications, PR, or reputation management for a major corporation. The commerce + media combination is rare and directly addresses what these teams need.
2. You want to work at the intersection of marketing and content — as a brand storyteller, content strategist, or integrated campaigns manager. Media theory plus marketing training is the ideal preparation.
3. You want to work in government communications or policy communications. The commercial grounding from BCom combined with media knowledge is the exact profile government communications units seek.

For all other career paths — management consulting, general management, HR, operations, strategy — the pure Management major (Course 4) is more efficient. Spending 50 breadth points on media subjects when you want a consulting career is an opportunity cost, as those points could go to Economics, Computing, Law, or other subjects that strengthen analytical and commercial credentials.

**Choose Course 4 (Management only) if:** Your target is consulting, general management, HR, operations, or strategy.

**Choose Course 3 (Management + Media breadth) if:** Your target is corporate communications, integrated marketing, PR, or any role that sits at the intersection of commercial and communications functions.

---

### BCom + International Relations Breadth (Course 5) vs. Bachelor of Arts Media and Communications (Course 6): Which Has Broader Scope?

Course 5 (BCom + IR breadth) has broader long-term career scope for the following reasons:

Commerce training opens doors that a pure arts degree does not — investment banking, consulting, FMCG, and corporate strategy are all accessible to a BCom graduate, but very few of these roles actively recruit B-ARTS graduates. The IR breadth adds a distinctive differentiator without sacrificing commercial employability.

The B-ARTS Media (Course 6) has scope that is more sector-specific — it is deep within media, communications, and journalism but narrow in cross-sector commercial applicability. The journalism market is structurally declining. The PR/communications market is growing but has a lower salary ceiling than most BCom career paths.

Where Course 6 wins over Course 5 is in direct entry to journalism and specialist media roles. An employer at the ABC or Guardian Australia looking for a journalist or content producer sees the B-ARTS + Media major as a better credential than a BCom with IR breadth. For this specific career pathway, Course 6 is more appropriate.

**Choose Course 5 if:** You want the maximum career optionality across commercial, diplomatic, government, and international business sectors.

**Choose Course 6 if:** You are certain you want to work in journalism, media production, or communications in a media-sector-specific environment.

## Final Note on Course Selection

The six configurations listed in the original brief reflect a coherent set of interests — commercial, digital, international, and communications — that are all achievable within the University of Melbourne's structure, albeit through a different architectural model than formal double degrees. Prospective students should:

1. Confirm with the University of Melbourne admissions office and Faculty of Business and Economics whether any new double degree structures have been announced for 2026 or 2027, given the ongoing discussion about "Melbourne Model 2.0" reforms.
2. Verify the 2026 specialisation subject list when it is published in full, as the Digital Marketing and Digital Business-adjacent specialisations are newly announced and details may evolve.
3. Consider postgraduate study as a deliberate part of the career plan — many of the most competitive career paths in consulting, diplomacy, and journalism are better served by a BCom + targeted master's degree than by any single undergraduate configuration alone.
